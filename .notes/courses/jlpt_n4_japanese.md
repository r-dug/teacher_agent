**Content:**

At N5, students used polite ます-form exclusively. N4 requires mastery of plain (dictionary/casual) form, which is the foundation for dozens of grammar patterns.

Plain present positive = dictionary form:
- Group 1: ends in う-column — かく, のむ, はなす, まつ, かう
- Group 2: ends in -る — たべる, みる, おきる, ねる
- Group 3: する, くる

Plain present negative (ない-form):
- Group 1: Change final う-column to あ-column + ない
  かく→かかない, のむ→のまない, はなす→はなさない, まつ→またない
  Exception: う ending → わない (NOT あない): かう→かわない, あう→あわない
- Group 2: Drop る, add ない: たべる→たべない, みる→みない
- Group 3: する→しない, くる→こない
- Special: ある→ない (NOT あらない)

Plain past positive (た-form):
Same sound changes as て-form but with た/だ:
かく→かいた, のむ→のんだ, はなす→はなした, まつ→まった, かう→かった
たべる→たべた, みる→みた, する→した, くる→きた
いく→いった (irregular, same as て-form)

Plain past negative:
ない→なかった: かかない→かかなかった, たべない→たべなかった, しない→しなかった

Summary table:
|           | Positive | Negative       |
|-----------|----------|----------------|
| Present   | かく      | かかない        |
| Past      | かいた    | かかなかった     |

**Key Concepts:**
- Form ない-form for all three verb groups: Group 1 う→あ+ない, Group 2 drop る+ない, Group 3 しない/こない
- Know the う→わない exception: かう→かわない, あう→あわない
- Know ある→ない (special case)
- Form た-form using て-form sound change rules with た/だ
- Form past negative: ない→なかった
- Vocabulary: かわる (change), きまる (be decided), つづける (continue), やめる (quit/stop), なくす (lose something), みつける (find), こわれる (break — intransitive), なおす (fix/correct), おくれる (be late), まにあう (be in time)

い-adjective plain forms:
|           | Positive     | Negative         |
|-----------|-------------|------------------|
| Present   | たかい       | たかくない        |
| Past      | たかかった    | たかくなかった     |

These are the same as N5 — い-adjectives are inherently plain. Adding です makes them polite.
いい remains irregular: よくない, よかった, よくなかった

な-adjective plain forms:
|           | Positive     | Negative            |
|-----------|-------------|---------------------|
| Present   | しずかだ     | しずかじゃない       |
| Past      | しずかだった  | しずかじゃなかった    |

Polite equivalents: しずかです, しずかじゃありません, しずかでした, しずかじゃありませんでした

Noun plain forms (same pattern as な-adjectives):
|           | Positive       | Negative              |
|-----------|---------------|----------------------|
| Present   | がくせいだ     | がくせいじゃない       |
| Past      | がくせいだった  | がくせいじゃなかった    |

When to use plain form:
1. Casual conversation with friends, family, people your age or younger
2. Inside grammatical structures (embedded clauses, と思う, etc.)
3. Written language (essays, diaries, novels)
4. Internal thought

Mixing politeness levels: The final sentence-ending form determines politeness. Embedded clauses use plain form even in polite speech:
あした あめが ふると おもいます。(polite — ends with おもいます)
The embedded clause あめが ふる is plain form inside a polite sentence.

**Key Concepts:**
- Form plain present/past/negative for な-adjectives: だ, じゃない, だった, じゃなかった
- Form plain present/past/negative for nouns: same as な-adjectives
- Understand that embedded clauses use plain form even in polite speech
- Know when to use plain vs polite form based on social context
- Vocabulary: ふつう (normal/ordinary), とくに (especially), たしか (if I recall correctly), もちろん (of course), やっぱり/やはり (as expected/after all), なるほど (I see/indeed), けっこう (fairly/quite), ぜひ (by all means), たぶん (probably), きっと (surely/certainly)

## Section 3: Casual Speech Patterns & Sentence-ending Particles

**Content:**

In casual speech, several changes occur beyond just dropping です/ます:

Dropping particles (very common in spoken Japanese):
ごはん(を) たべた？— Did you eat?
これ(は) おいしいね。— This is delicious, right?
どこ(に) いく？— Where are you going?
Particles most often dropped: を, は, に (destination), が (sometimes). Never drop: で (location), から, まで, の.

Casual question — drop か, use rising intonation:
あした いく？↑ — Are you going tomorrow? (instead of あした いきますか)

の at sentence end — explanation/seeking explanation:
どうしたの？— What happened? / What's wrong?
あした しけんなの。— (The thing is,) I have an exam tomorrow.
For nouns/な-adj, use なの: びょうきなの？— Are you sick?

だ vs です in questions:
With か: Polite: がくせいですか。 Plain: がくせい？(NOT がくせいだか)
だ is NOT used before か or の in questions. がくせいなの？(NOT がくせいだの)

Casual sentence-ending particles:
- ね — seeking agreement (same as polite): いいてんきだね
- よ — asserting: もう おそいよ — It's already late, you know
- よね — confirming shared knowledge: あした やすみだよね — Tomorrow's a day off, right?
- っけ — trying to remember: なまえ、なんだっけ？— What was your name again?
- かな — wondering: だいじょうぶかな — I wonder if it's OK
- な (male, to self) — あした がんばるな — I'll do my best tomorrow (self-talk)
- わ (softening, all genders in modern usage): もう いくわ — I'm going now

**Key Concepts:**
- Drop particles naturally in casual speech: を, は, に are commonly omitted
- Form casual questions with rising intonation instead of か
- Use の/なの for explanation/seeking explanation
- Know that だ is dropped before か and の in questions
- Use casual particles: っけ (remembering), かな (wondering), な (self-reflection)
- Vocabulary: どうした (what happened — casual), まじで (seriously — slang), やっと (finally/at last), そろそろ (soon/before long), だいたい (roughly/approximately), とにかく (anyway/in any case), ちゃんと (properly), しっかり (firmly/properly)

## Section 4: Giving & Reporting Reasons — から & ので

**Content:**
TEACHING APPROACH — Vocab first, then grammar, then drill.
STEP 1 — VOCABULARY: Before explaining any grammar, introduce the vocabulary listed in Key Concepts. Say each word, have the student repeat, give the meaning. Then rapid-fire quiz with start_timer (60 seconds): say English, student says Japanese. Reverse: say Japanese, student says English. Do not proceed until the student can recall all new words.
STEP 2 — TEACH: Explain the grammar point using the content below. After each new pattern, immediately have the student produce 2-3 example sentences using it. Do not lecture for more than 2-3 sentences before requiring student production.
STEP 3 — DRILL: After teaching all patterns in this section, use start_timer for a 2-minute comprehensive drill. Mix all patterns from this section: give English sentences or prompts, student produces Japanese. Include vocabulary from this AND previous sections for cumulative review.

から (kara) — because/since (subjective reason, often personal):
Plain form + から:
あたまが いたいから、きょうは やすみます。— Because my head hurts, I'll take today off.
じかんが ないから、タクシーで いこう。— Since there's no time, let's take a taxi.

In polite speech, ます/です can precede から:
あたまが いたいですから、きょうは やすみます。(polite throughout)

ので (node) — because/since (objective reason, softer/more polite):
Plain form + ので (but な-adj/noun: な + ので, NOT だ + ので):
あしたは しけんなので、こんばん べんきょうします。— Since there's an exam tomorrow, I'll study tonight.
でんしゃが おくれたので、ちこくしました。— Because the train was late, I was late.

から vs ので:
- から: more subjective, stronger, can sound blunt. Often for personal reasons or commands.
- ので: more objective, softer, more polite. Preferred in formal situations and with superiors.
- For requests/suggestions, ので is preferred: あめなので、かさを もっていってください。

Both can come at the end to explain (trailing reason):
どうして やすんだんですか？— Why were you absent?
ちょっと ぐあいが わるかったので...。— Because I wasn't feeling well...

**Key Concepts:**
- Form reason clauses with から: [plain/polite form] + から + result
- Form reason clauses with ので: [plain form] + ので + result (な-adj/noun use な before ので)
- Distinguish から (subjective/strong) from ので (objective/softer/more polite)
- Use trailing reason with ので for polite explanations
- Vocabulary: ちこくする (be late/tardy), ぐあいがわるい (feel unwell), ねつがある (have a fever), つかれる (get tired), いそがしい (busy), ひま (free/not busy), じゅんびする (prepare), よういする (prepare/arrange), けっせきする (be absent), しゅっせきする (attend)

[plain form] + と おもいます — I think that...

あした あめが ふると おもいます。— I think it will rain tomorrow.
この えいがは おもしろいと おもいます。— I think this movie is interesting.
たなかさんは こないと おもいます。— I think Tanaka won't come.

For first person ("I think I will..."), use つもり or ～ようと思う (see Lesson 5) rather than ～と思う:
Not ideal: あした いくと おもいます。(sounds like you're uncertain about your own plans)
Better: あした いくつもりです。

Past thought: ～と おもいました
やさしいと おもいました。— I thought it was easy.

Negative thought — two patterns with different meanings:
- ～ないと おもう: I think [not X] — あめは ふらないと おもいます (I think it won't rain)
- ～と おもわない: I don't think [X] — less common, stronger denial

Quoting others' thoughts: [person] は ～と おもっています
たなかさんは にほんごが かんたんだと おもっています。— Tanaka thinks Japanese is easy.
Note: おもっています (not おもいます) for ongoing beliefs of others.

**Key Concepts:**
- Form opinion expressions: [plain form]とおもいます
- Use noun/な-adj + だ before と: かんたんだとおもう, がくせいだとおもう
- Distinguish ～ないとおもう (think not) from ～とおもわない (don't think)
- Use おもっています for others' ongoing thoughts
- Vocabulary: たぶん (probably), きっと (surely), もしかしたら (perhaps), ～かもしれない (might be), ぜったい (absolutely), かならず (without fail), ほんとうに (really/truly), ～のように (like/as if)

[plain form] + と いいます/いいました — [someone] says/said that...

せんせいは あした テストが あると いいました。— The teacher said there's a test tomorrow.
ともだちは にほんの たべものは おいしいと いっていました。— My friend was saying that Japanese food is delicious.

Direct quote (exact words) vs indirect quote:
Direct: たなかさんは「あした いきます」と いいました。— Tanaka said, "I'll go tomorrow."
Indirect: たなかさんは あした いくと いいました。— Tanaka said he'll go tomorrow.

という (to iu) — "called" / "that says" / used to define or name:
「すみません」は えいごで なんと いいますか。— How do you say "sumimasen" in English?

「花見」というのは、さくらを みることです。— "Hanami" means viewing cherry blossoms.

～と いう + noun (noun modifier):
たなかという ひと — a person called Tanaka
「ノルウェイの もり」という ほん — a book called "Norwegian Wood"

そうです (hearsay) — I heard that / they say that:
[plain form] + そうです
あした あめが ふるそうです。— I heard it will rain tomorrow. / They say it'll rain tomorrow.
あの レストランは おいしいそうです。— I hear that restaurant is good.
Note: This is different from ～そうです (appearance) covered later.

- Form reported speech: [plain form]といいました

## Section 3: Noun-modifying Clauses

**Content:**

STEP 1 — VOCABULARY: Before explaining any grammar, introduce the vocabulary listed in Key Concepts. Say each word, have the student repeat, give the meaning. Then rapid-fire quiz with start_timer (60 seconds): say English, student says Japanese. Reverse: say Japanese, student says English. Do not proceed until the student can recall all new words.

In Japanese, modifying clauses come BEFORE the noun they modify — the opposite of English relative clauses ("the book that I bought" → "I bought + book"):

わたしが きのう かった ほん — the book that I bought yesterday
ともだちが すんでいる まち — the town where my friend lives
あそこで はしっている ひと — the person running over there

The modifying clause uses plain form and directly precedes the noun — no relative pronoun ("that," "which," "who") is needed:

English: The movie [that we watched yesterday] was interesting.
Japanese: [きのう みた] えいがは おもしろかったです。

English: The person [who is wearing a red shirt] is my teacher.
Japanese: [あかい シャツを きている] ひとは わたしの せんせいです。

English: The restaurant [where I always eat] is near the station.
Japanese: [いつも たべる] レストランは えきの ちかくにあります。

More complex examples:
おかあさんが つくった りょうりは おいしいです。— The food that Mom made is delicious.
きのう あった ことを はなしてください。— Please tell me about what happened yesterday.
にほんで かいたい ものが たくさん あります。— There are many things I want to buy in Japan.

こと and もの as generic nouns:
～こと — abstract thing/fact: きのう あった こと (what happened yesterday)
～もの — concrete thing: たべたい もの (something I want to eat)

**Key Concepts:**
- Place modifying clauses in plain form directly before the noun: [clause] + noun
- No relative pronoun needed — Japanese uses word order instead
- Use こと for abstract things/facts and もの for concrete things
- Modify nouns with complex clauses: [きのう ともだちと いった] レストラン
- Vocabulary: りゆう (reason), ばしょ (place), よてい (plan/schedule), けいけん (experience), きかい (opportunity/chance), ないよう (contents), いけん (opinion), もくてき (purpose/goal), けっか (result), じょうほう (information)

## Section 4: んです / のです — Explanation & Context

**Content:**
TEACHING APPROACH — Vocab first, then grammar, then drill.
STEP 1 — VOCABULARY: Before explaining any grammar, introduce the vocabulary listed in Key Concepts. Say each word, have the student repeat, give the meaning. Then rapid-fire quiz with start_timer (60 seconds): say English, student says Japanese. Reverse: say Japanese, student says English. Do not proceed until the student can recall all new words.
STEP 2 — TEACH: Explain the grammar point using the content below. After each new pattern, immediately have the student produce 2-3 example sentences using it. Do not lecture for more than 2-3 sentences before requiring student production.
STEP 3 — DRILL: After teaching all patterns in this section, use start_timer for a 2-minute comprehensive drill. Mix all patterns from this section: give English sentences or prompts, student produces Japanese. Include vocabulary from this AND previous sections for cumulative review.

～んです (spoken) / ～のです (written/formal) adds explanatory nuance — "the thing is..." / "it's that..." / seeking or providing context.

Formation:
- Verb plain form + んです: いくんです, いかないんです, いったんです
- い-adjective + んです: たかいんです, たかくないんです
- な-adjective + なんです: しずかなんです (NOT しずかだんです)
- Noun + なんです: びょうきなんです

Usage 1 — Seeking explanation (with question):
どうして おくれたんですか。— Why were you late? (I noticed you're late — explain)
Compare: どうして おくれましたか。(neutral question, no implicit "I noticed")

Usage 2 — Providing explanation/background:
きょうは はやく かえるんです。— (The thing is,) I'm going home early today.
(Implies there's a reason — maybe you have an appointment)

あたまが いたいんです。— (The thing is,) I have a headache.
(Volunteering the reason for looking unwell, leaving early, etc.)

Usage 3 — Softening requests:
すみません、みちを おしえてほしいんですが...。— Excuse me, I'd like you to tell me the way...
(んですが trailing off = polite indirect request)

Common mistake: Overusing んです. Use it only when there's implicit context, explanation, or background — not for every statement.

**Key Concepts:**
- Form んです with verbs/い-adj + んです, な-adj/nouns + なんです
- Use んですか to seek explanation for something you've noticed
- Use んです to provide background context or reasons
- Use んですが... for softened indirect requests
- Vocabulary: じつは (actually/the truth is), つまり (in other words), たとえば (for example), ところで (by the way), それに (moreover/besides), しかし (however — formal), ただ (however/just), そのため (for that reason)

# Lesson 3 — Verb Forms Expansion

## Section 1: Potential Form — "Can Do"

**Content:**
TEACHING APPROACH — Vocab first, then grammar, then drill.
STEP 1 — VOCABULARY: Before explaining any grammar, introduce the vocabulary listed in Key Concepts. Say each word, have the student repeat, give the meaning. Then rapid-fire quiz with start_timer (60 seconds): say English, student says Japanese. Reverse: say Japanese, student says English. Do not proceed until the student can recall all new words.
STEP 2 — TEACH: Explain the grammar point using the content below. After each new pattern, immediately have the student produce 2-3 example sentences using it. Do not lecture for more than 2-3 sentences before requiring student production.
STEP 3 — DRILL: After teaching all patterns in this section, use start_timer for a 2-minute comprehensive drill. Mix all patterns from this section: give English sentences or prompts, student produces Japanese. Include vocabulary from this AND previous sections for cumulative review.

N5 used ～ことができる for ability. N4 introduces the shorter potential form:

Group 1: Change final う-column to え-column + る
かく→かける (can write), のむ→のめる (can drink), はなす→はなせる (can speak)
まつ→まてる (can wait), かう→かえる (can buy)

Group 2: Drop る, add られる
たべる→たべられる (can eat), みる→みられる (can see)
Colloquial shortening (ら抜き — ra-nuki): たべれる, みれる — very common in speech but considered incorrect in formal/written Japanese and on tests.

Group 3:
する→できる, くる→こられる (colloquial: これる)

All potential forms conjugate as Group 2 (ru-verbs):
かける→かけます, かけない, かけた, かけなかった

Particle change: With potential form, を often becomes が:
すしを たべます → すしが たべられます (I can eat sushi)
にほんごを はなします → にほんごが はなせます (I can speak Japanese)
Both を and が are acceptable, but が is more standard with potential.

みえる (can be seen/is visible) vs みられる (can watch/have the ability to see):
ここから ふじさんが みえます。— Mt. Fuji is visible from here. (natural ability)
いまは テレビが みられません。— I can't watch TV now. (circumstantial ability)

きこえる (can be heard/is audible) vs きける (can listen):
おんがくが きこえます。— I can hear music. (natural)
ここで おんがくが きけます。— You can listen to music here. (circumstantial)

**Key Concepts:**
- Form potential for Group 1: う-column → え-column + る (かく→かける)
- Form potential for Group 2: drop る + られる (たべる→たべられる)
- Form potential for Group 3: する→できる, くる→こられる
- Use が (not を) with potential form: にほんごがはなせます
- Distinguish みえる/きこえる (natural) from みられる/きける (circumstantial)
- Vocabulary: よやくできる (can reserve), もうしこめる (can apply), さんかできる (can participate), しんじられる (can believe), がまんできる (can endure), りかいできる (can understand), うんてんできる (can drive), そうだんできる (can consult)

## Section 2: Volitional Form — "Let's / I'll"

Japanese has multiple conditional forms. N4 focuses on ば-form and たら-form:

ば-form (if... then — general/hypothetical condition):
- Group 1: Change final う-column to え-column + ば
  かく→かけば, のむ→のめば, いく→いけば
- Group 2: Drop る, add れば: たべる→たべれば, みる→みれば
- Group 3: する→すれば, くる→くれば
- い-adjective: replace い with ければ: たかい→たかければ, いい→よければ
- な-adjective/noun: ～であれば or ～なら(ば)
- Negative: ～なければ: いかなければ (if [one] doesn't go)

たら-form (if/when — completed condition):
た-form + ら: いったら, たべたら, したら, きたら
- い-adj: たかかったら, よかったら
- な-adj/noun: しずかだったら, がくせいだったら
- Negative: ～なかったら: いかなかったら

ば vs たら:
- ば: More hypothetical/general. Often for conditions that may or may not happen.
  やすければ、かいます。— If it's cheap, I'll buy it.
- たら: More concrete/temporal. Often implies "when X happens/happened."
  うちに かえったら、でんわしてください。— When you get home, please call me.
- たら is more versatile and common in conversation. When in doubt, use たら.

Important restriction: ば-form cannot have a volitional/request main clause when the subject is the same:
Wrong: えきに いけば、でんわしてください。
Right: えきに いったら、でんわしてください。— When you get to the station, please call.

よかったら — "if you'd like" (polite softener):
よかったら、いっしょに いきませんか。— If you'd like, would you like to go together?

**Key Concepts:**
- Form ば-conditional: Group 1 う→えば, Group 2 る→れば, い-adj い→ければ
- Form たら-conditional: た-form + ら for all word types
- Distinguish ば (hypothetical) from たら (concrete/temporal)
- Know ば cannot precede volitional/request clauses with same subject
- Use よかったら as a polite softener
- Vocabulary: まにあう (make it in time), おくれる (be late), うまくいく (go well), しっぱいする (fail), せいこうする (succeed), あんしんする (feel relieved), きをつける (be careful), むりをする (overdo it)

と (to) — natural/automatic consequence ("whenever X, Y always happens"):
[present plain form] + と:
このボタンを おすと、ドアが あきます。— When you press this button, the door opens.
はるに なると、さくらが さきます。— When spring comes, cherry blossoms bloom.
みぎに まがると、えきが あります。— If you turn right, there's a station.

Rules for と:
- Main clause must be non-volitional (no requests, suggestions, or intentions)
- Describes natural/predictable consequences, habitual sequences, or giving directions
- Wrong: あした てんきが いいと、こうえんに いきましょう。(volitional — use たら)
- Past discoveries: ドアを あけると、ねこが いた。— When I opened the door, there was a cat.

なら (nara) — "if it's the case that..." (contextual/topical condition):
[plain form] + なら: いくなら, やすいなら, がくせいなら
にほんに いくなら、きょうとに いったほうがいいですよ。— If you're going to Japan, you should visit Kyoto.
あまいものが すきなら、このケーキを たべてみてください。— If you like sweet things, try this cake.

なら is unique because:
- It picks up on something the listener said or implied
- It gives advice/suggestions based on that condition
- The advice can happen BEFORE the condition: にほんに いくなら、パスポートを よういしてね。

Summary of all four conditionals:
| Form | Nuance | Main clause restriction |
|------|--------|------------------------|
| ば   | Hypothetical/general | No volitional (same subject) |
| たら  | Concrete/temporal | None |
| と   | Automatic/natural | No volitional at all |
| なら  | Contextual/topical | None |

**Key Concepts:**
- Form と-conditional: [present plain] + と for automatic/natural consequences
- Know と cannot have volitional main clauses
- Form なら-conditional: [plain] + なら for contextual "if that's the case" conditions
- Distinguish all four conditionals: ば, たら, と, なら
- Vocabulary: おす (push/press), ひく (pull), まわす (turn/rotate), ボタン (button), スイッチ (switch), もし (if — used with ば/たら for emphasis), パスポート (passport), ビザ (visa), にゅうがく (enrollment/admission), そつぎょう (graduation)

Japanese uses three verbs for giving/receiving, based on perspective and social direction:

あげる — I/someone gives (to someone else, outward direction):
わたしは ともだちに プレゼントを あげました。— I gave my friend a present.
やまださんは たなかさんに ほんを あげました。— Yamada gave Tanaka a book.

もらう — I/someone receives (from someone, inward direction):
わたしは ともだちに/から プレゼントを もらいました。— I received a present from my friend.
たなかさんは やまださんに/から ほんを もらいました。— Tanaka received a book from Yamada.

くれる — Someone gives (to me/my in-group, inward from giver's perspective):
ともだちが (わたしに) プレゼントを くれました。— My friend gave me a present.
せんせいが むすめに ほんを くれました。— The teacher gave my daughter a book.

あげる vs くれる — both mean "give" but:
- あげる: Giver is subject, recipient is not the speaker's in-group
- くれる: Giver is subject, recipient IS the speaker or in-group (implies gratitude)

Hierarchy-sensitive alternatives:
- あげる → さしあげる (humble, giving to superiors)
- もらう → いただく (humble, receiving from superiors)
- くれる → くださる (honorific, superior gives to me)

わたしは せんせいに おみやげを さしあげました。— I gave my teacher a souvenir. (humble)
せんせいが わたしに ほんを くださいました。— My teacher gave me a book. (honorific)

**Key Concepts:**
- Use あげる when giving to others (outward): [giver]は[recipient]にあげる
- Use もらう when receiving (inward): [receiver]は[giver]に/からもらう
- Use くれる when someone gives to me/my group: [giver]が(わたしに)くれる
- Know honorific variants: さしあげる, いただく, くださる
- Vocabulary: プレゼント (present), おみやげ (souvenir), おくりもの (gift), はなたば (bouquet), てがみ (letter), にもつ (luggage/package), きっぷ (ticket), チケット (ticket), じしょ (dictionary), アドバイス (advice)

Combining て-form with あげる/もらう/くれる expresses doing things FOR someone:

～てあげる — I do something for someone (outward favor):

Japanese has a hierarchy of request politeness. From casual to very polite:

～て (casual command): まって — Wait.
～てくれ (casual request, mainly male): まってくれ — Wait for me.
～てくれない？(casual request): まってくれない？— Won't you wait?
～てください (polite request — N5): まってください — Please wait.
～てくれませんか (polite — "won't you?"): まってくれませんか — Won't you wait for me?
～てもらえませんか (more polite — "could I have you?"): まってもらえませんか — Could you wait?
～ていただけませんか (very polite/humble): まっていただけませんか — Would you be so kind as to wait?

The negative question form (～ませんか) is more polite than the positive because it gives the listener room to refuse.

Common polite request patterns:
すみません、しゃしんを とってもらえませんか。— Excuse me, could you take a photo?
このかんじの よみかたを おしえていただけませんか。— Could you teach me how to read this kanji?
もうすこし ゆっくり はなしてもらえませんか。— Could you speak a bit more slowly?

Granting/declining:
はい、いいですよ。— Sure, no problem.
すみません、ちょっと...。— Sorry, that's a bit... (soft refusal)
もうしわけありませんが、いま ちょっと...。— I'm terribly sorry, but right now... (very polite refusal)

**Key Concepts:**
- Order the request hierarchy from casual to formal: て→てくれ→てください→てくれませんか→てもらえませんか→ていただけませんか
- Use negative question forms for more polite requests
- Use すみません or もうしわけありませんが to soften requests
- Decline politely with trailing ちょっと...
- Vocabulary: もうしわけない (inexcusable/very sorry), おそれいりますが (I'm afraid/excuse me but), さしつかえなければ (if you don't mind), よろしければ (if it's alright with you), きにしないで (don't worry about it)

The passive voice reverses who does what: "X was done (by Y)."

Formation:
- Group 1: Change final う-column to あ-column + れる
  かく→かかれる, のむ→のまれる, とる→とられる
- Group 2: Drop る, add られる: たべる→たべられる, みる→みられる
- Group 3: する→される, くる→こられる

Direct passive (action done to the subject):
わたしは せんせいに ほめられました。— I was praised by the teacher.

わたしは あめに ふられた。— I got rained on. (rain fell and I suffered)
でんしゃで あしを ふまれた。— Someone stepped on my foot on the train.
よなかに あかちゃんに なかれた。— The baby cried at night (and it bothered me).

Particle patterns:
- Direct passive: [subject]は [agent]に ～れる

- Form passive: Group 1 う→あれる, Group 2 る→られる, する→される, くる→こられる

- Understand indirect/adversative passive: suffering from someone's action
- Use に to mark the agent (doer) in passive sentences
- Vocabulary: ほめる (praise), しかる (scold), ぬすむ (steal), ふむ (step on), なぐる (hit/punch), だます (deceive), わらう (laugh), よぶ (call), えらぶ (choose/select), しょうたいする (invite — formal)

The causative expresses making or letting someone do something:

Formation:
- Group 1: Change final う-column to あ-column + せる

1. 丁寧語 (ていねいご) — polite: です/ます (already known)
2. 尊敬語 (そんけいご) — honorific: elevates the listener/subject's actions
3. 謙譲語 (けんじょうご) — humble: lowers the speaker's actions

Common honorific verbs (used for others' actions):
| Plain      | Honorific          | Meaning        |

| する       | なさる            | do             |
| しっている | ごぞんじだ        | know           |
| くれる     | くださる          | give (to me)   |

Common humble verbs (used for own actions):
| Plain      | Humble            | Meaning        |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------|
| いく/くる  | まいる            | go/come        |
| いる       | おる              | be             |
| たべる/のむ | いただく         | eat/drink      |
| いう       | もうす            | say            |
| みる       | はいけんする      | see/look       |

[て-form] + も — "even if / even though":
あめが ふっても、さんぽに いきます。— Even if it rains, I'll go for a walk.
たかくても、かいたいです。— Even if it's expensive, I want to buy it.
やすくても、いらない。— Even if it's cheap, I don't want it.

Formation:
- Verb: て-form + も: いっても, たべても, しても
- い-adjective: くても: たかくても, さむくても, よくても (from いい)
- な-adjective: でも: しずかでも, べんりでも
- Noun: でも: あめでも, にちようびでも

いくら/どんなに + ～ても — "no matter how much":
いくら たべても、ふとらない。— No matter how much I eat, I don't gain weight.
どんなに いそがしくても、あさごはんは たべる。— No matter how busy I am, I eat breakfast.

Question word + ～ても — "no matter who/what/where/when":
だれが きても、おなじです。— No matter who comes, it's the same.
なにを たべても、おいしい。— No matter what I eat, it's delicious.
どこに いっても、にほんごを べんきょうします。— Wherever I go, I study Japanese.

～ても いい was covered in N5 (permission). Here ても is used for concessive meaning.

**Key Concepts:**
- Form concessive ても: verb て-form+も, い-adj くても, な-adj/noun でも
- Use いくら/どんなに+ても for "no matter how much"
- Use question word+ても for "no matter who/what/where/when"
- Vocabulary: ふとる (gain weight), やせる (lose weight), あきらめる (give up), つづける (continue), がんばる (do one's best), たえる (endure), へいき (fine/unfazed), だいじょうぶ (alright/okay)

**Content:**

[plain form] + のに — "although / despite / even though" (with a nuance of frustration, disappointment, or surprise):

やくそくしたのに、こなかった。— Even though we promised, they didn't come.
たくさん べんきょうしたのに、テストに おちた。— Despite studying a lot, I failed the test.
きのうは あつかったのに、きょうは さむい。— Yesterday was hot, but today it's cold.

Formation (same as のです/んです):
- Verb: plain form + のに: いくのに, いったのに, いかないのに
- い-adjective: plain + のに: たかいのに
- な-adjective: な + のに: しずかなのに (NOT しずかだのに)
- Noun: な + のに: やすみなのに

のに vs ても:
- のに: States a FACT and expresses frustration/surprise that the result didn't match expectations
  "I studied, yet I failed" — the studying actually happened, the failure is disappointing
- ても: Hypothetical or general concession
  "Even if I study, I'll fail" — could be hypothetical

のに vs けど/が:
- のに carries emotional weight (frustration, disappointment, complaint)
- けど/が is neutral: いったけど、いなかった (I went, but they weren't there — neutral)
- のに adds feeling: いったのに、いなかった (I went, and yet they weren't there! — frustrated)

のに can trail off at the end for implied complaint:
せっかく つくったのに...。— I went through the trouble of making it, and yet... (implies it was wasted)

**Key Concepts:**
- Form のに: verb/い-adj + のに, な-adj/noun + なのに
- Understand のに implies frustration, disappointment, or surprise
- Distinguish のに (emotional, factual) from ても (hypothetical/general)
- Use trailing のに for implied complaint
- Vocabulary: せっかく (with effort/trouble), むだにする (waste), おちる (fail — exam), うかる (pass — exam), まける (lose), かつ (win), やくそく (promise), きたいする (expect), がっかりする (be disappointed), くやしい (frustrating/vexing)

[plain form] + し — "and what's more" / "not only... but also" (listing multiple reasons or qualities):

この レストランは やすいし、おいしいし、ちかいし、よく いきます。
— This restaurant is cheap, delicious, and close, so I go often.

あしたは テストが あるし、レポートも ださなきゃいけないし、いそがしい。
— Tomorrow I have a test and I also have to submit a report, so I'm busy.

Formation:
- Verb: plain form + し: いくし, いったし, いかないし
- い-adjective: plain + し: たかいし, たかくないし
- な-adjective: だし: しずかだし, しずかじゃないし
- Noun: だし: にちようびだし

Key characteristics:
- Lists multiple parallel reasons (doesn't need to be exhaustive)
- Often leads to a concluding statement
- Can have just one し clause (implying there are other reasons too):
  あたまも いたいし、きょうは やすみます。— My head hurts (among other things), so I'll take today off.

し vs から:
- から gives ONE specific reason: あたまが いたいから、やすみます。
- し implies MULTIPLE reasons (even if only one is stated): あたまも いたいし...

し for listing positive qualities:
やまださんは あたまが いいし、やさしいし、かっこいいし、すてきです。
— Yamada is smart, kind, and handsome — wonderful.

**Key Concepts:**
- Form し clauses: verb/い-adj plain+し, な-adj/noun+だし
- Use し to list multiple reasons or qualities
- Understand single し implies additional unstated reasons
- Distinguish し (multiple reasons) from から (single reason)
- Vocabulary: それに (moreover), その上 (on top of that), しかも (furthermore), かっこいい (cool/handsome), すてき (wonderful), さいあく (the worst), さいこう (the best), ひどい (terrible/awful), すばらしい (wonderful/magnificent)

## Section 4: ～たり～たりする — Non-exhaustive Action Listing

**Content:**
TEACHING APPROACH — Vocab first, then grammar, then drill.
STEP 1 — VOCABULARY: Before explaining any grammar, introduce the vocabulary listed in Key Concepts. Say each word, have the student repeat, give the meaning. Then rapid-fire quiz with start_timer (60 seconds): say English, student says Japanese. Reverse: say Japanese, student says English. Do not proceed until the student can recall all new words.
STEP 2 — TEACH: Explain the grammar point using the content below. After each new pattern, immediately have the student produce 2-3 example sentences using it. Do not lecture for more than 2-3 sentences before requiring student production.
STEP 3 — DRILL: After teaching all patterns in this section, use start_timer for a 2-minute comprehensive drill. Mix all patterns from this section: give English sentences or prompts, student produces Japanese. Include vocabulary from this AND previous sections for cumulative review.

[た-form] + り ... [た-form] + り する — "do things like X and Y":

やすみの ひは ほんを よんだり、えいがを みたりします。
— On days off, I do things like read books and watch movies.

きのうは そうじしたり、せんたくしたり しました。
— Yesterday I did things like cleaning and laundry.

Formation: た-form + り for each verb, end with する (conjugated for tense/politeness):
いく→いったり, たべる→たべたり, する→したり, くる→きたり

The final する carries the tense:
- Present: ～たり～たりします
- Past: ～たり～たりしました
- Negative: ～たり～たりしません

Can also be used with adjectives for alternating states:
てんきは あつかったり さむかったり します。— The weather alternates between hot and cold.
びょうきの ちょうしは よかったり わるかったり です。— The illness varies between better and worse.

Typically uses two ～たり clauses, but three is acceptable. One is also possible:
にちようびは、テレビを みたりします。— On Sundays, I do things like watch TV.
(Implies watching TV is one of several activities)

たり vs て-form chaining:
- て-form: sequential actions — おきて、たべて、いきました (got up, ate, went)
- たり: non-sequential, sampling — よんだり、みたりしました (things like reading and watching)

**Key Concepts:**
- Form ～たり～たりする: [た-form]+り…[た-form]+りする
- Understand this lists representative/non-exhaustive actions
- Apply to adjectives for alternating states: あつかったりさむかったり
- Distinguish from て-form chaining (sequential vs sampling)
- Vocabulary: ゲームをする (play games), ジョギングする (jog), ヨガをする (do yoga), ねっころがる (lie down/lounge), ぶらぶらする (wander/hang around), のんびりする (relax/take it easy), でかける (go out), ひるねする (take a nap)

Complete list of major N4 grammar patterns for review:

Plain form foundations:
- ～と思う (I think), ～と言う (say that), ～そうだ (hearsay)
- ～のです/んです (explanation), noun-modifying clauses

Conditionals (four types):
- ～ば (hypothetical), ～たら (concrete/temporal), ～と (automatic), ～なら (contextual)

Giving/receiving:
- あげる/もらう/くれる (objects)
- ～てあげる/～てもらう/～てくれる (favors)

Verb forms:
- Potential: かける, たべられる, できる
- Volitional: かこう, たべよう, しよう
- Passive: かかれる, たべられる, される
- Causative: かかせる, たべさせる, させる
- Causative-passive: かかされる, たべさせられる

Obligation & advice:
- ～なければならない/～なくてはいけない (must)
- ～なくてもいい (don't have to)
- ～たほうがいい/～ないほうがいい (should/shouldn't)

Change & decision:
- ～ようにする (try to), ～ようになる (come to)
- ～ことにする (decide to), ～ことになる (it's been decided)
- ～ようと思う (thinking of doing)

Appearance & conjecture:
- ～そう (appearance — stem+そう), ～そうだ (hearsay — plain+そうだ)
- ～らしい (apparently), ～みたい/～よう (seems like)
- ～かもしれない (might)

Complex sentences:
- ～ても (even if), ～のに (despite — with frustration)
- ～し (listing reasons), ～たり～たりする (listing actions)
- ～ので (because — soft), ～から (because — direct)

Honorifics:
- いらっしゃる, めしあがる, おっしゃる (honorific)
- まいる, いただく, もうす (humble)

Frequency trap: The most commonly tested N4 patterns are conditionals (especially たら), ～ても, ～のに, passive, ～ようになる, and ～てもらう/くれる.

**Key Concepts:**
- Review all four conditional forms and when to use each

## Section 4: Listening Strategies — Indirect Answers & Nuance

**Content:**

N4 listening is significantly harder than N5 because answers are often indirect:

Indirect refusal patterns:
- ちょっと... — "That's a bit..." (= no)
- かんがえておきます — "I'll think about it" (= probably no)
- その日は ちょっと よていが... — "That day I have plans..." (= can't)
- いけたら いくんですけど... — "If I could go I would, but..." (= probably can't)

Indirect agreement/positive patterns:
- それは いいですね — "That sounds good" (= yes)
- ぜひ — "By all means" (= enthusiastic yes)
- よろこんで — "With pleasure" (= yes)

Key listening skills for N4:

1. Track topic changes: Conversations may start discussing one thing, then shift. The question is usually about the final decision.
   A: 映画に行かない？ B: いいね！何時？ A: 3時は？ B: 3時はちょっと...4時なら。A: じゃ、4時に。
   Q: 何時に映画に行きますか。→ 4時 (not 3時)

2. Negation of negation: Pay attention to corrected information.
   A: 明日は会議、10時からでしたよね？ B: ああ、10時じゃなくて、11時に変わりましたよ。
   Q: 会議は何時から？→ 11時

3. Understanding ～ことになった (external decision) vs ～ことにした (personal decision) in context.

4. Catching ～てもらう/～てくれる to understand who is doing what for whom.

Practice note-taking: Write down numbers, times, and key nouns while listening. Use abbreviations: 月 for Monday, 3→ for 3:00.

**Key Concepts:**
- Recognize indirect refusal: ちょっと..., かんがえておきます
- Track topic changes and corrections — the final answer matters
- Listen for negation corrections: ～じゃなくて、～
- Take notes on numbers, times, and names during audio
- Vocabulary: ちょくせつ (directly), かんせつ (indirectly), ことわる (refuse), しょうちする (consent/agree), ていあんする (propose/suggest), へんこう (change — plans), かくにんする (confirm), とりけす (cancel)
